SPANISH AND MANDARIN -- RECAP SESSIONS (VERSION 158, LAST TIME'S FEATURE -- THE LAST PART OF YOUR LANGUAGE PRACTICE IDEA)

coverage and revision system you already have. That's exactly what this is: a new "📋 Recap"
button on the Language practice page, for both languages, sitting next to "A mix of everything".
HOW IT WORKS: tap it and it tells her, in plain terms, which topics are due another look and
which of her five games (fill in the blank, tap the words, translate the sentence, translate the
word, Repeat after me) she's currently weakest at. Tap "Start my recap" and she gets the usual
10-question round -- same scoring, same Mistake Dojo as every other game -- except the questions
are chosen on purpose: more of whatever topic needs revisiting, more of whatever game she's
struggling with, while still making sure all 10 of her level's sentences get covered, same
guarantee "a mix of everything" already gives. If a Back-and-forth conversation (last time's
feature) covers a topic that's also due, up to two "Replay" buttons for those appear too.
UNDER THE HOOD, FOR WHAT IT'S WORTH: I built this to genuinely reuse what you already have,
rather than bolt on something separate -- it leans on the exact same "is this topic overdue"
rule the Coverage map already uses, and the exact same five games, rather than inventing a sixth
one. That also means a wrong answer in a Recap round lands in the Mistake Dojo and gets trained
exactly the same way as a wrong answer anywhere else -- I didn't have to write any new code for
that part at all, and I checked that directly rather than assuming it.

SPANISH AND MANDARIN -- BACK-AND-FORTH CONVERSATIONS (VERSION 149, LAST TIME'S FEATURE -- THE PREVIOUS PART OF YOUR LANGUAGE PRACTICE IDEA)
This is the next piece of the Duolingo-style language practice you asked for ages ago -- part "e",
back-and-forth conversations, "to help her speak properly". There's a decision baked into how I built
it, so I want to be upfront about it straight away: a fully open, free-form conversation (where she can
type or say almost anything and the app replies naturally) would need a live AI service doing the
talking, which is a genuinely bigger decision -- it costs money per use, and it means sending whatever
she says to an outside AI company while she's mid-conversation, which is a privacy question, not just a
feature request. So for now I've built the SCRIPTED version: safe, free, and works entirely offline,
like everything else here.
HOW IT WORKS: a new "Back-and-forth conversations" button on the Language practice page, for both
languages. She picks a conversation (3 per language: meeting someone new, talking about her family, and
a third one built specifically around the Formal-and-informal lesson she already has -- asking a
stranger for help in Spanish, talking to a teacher in Mandarin). The other person speaks first (she can
tap to hear it), and she picks from 3 ways to reply -- her correct line, plus two wrong ones. One wrong
answer is usually something she ALREADY said earlier in the same conversation (so she has to actually
follow the back-and-forth, not just translate one sentence in isolation), and the third scenario in each
language specifically tests the polite/informal distinction (for example, in Spanish the wrong answer
says "Perdona" -- fine for a friend -- where the right one needs "Perdone", for a stranger). Whatever she
picks, the conversation then carries on with the CORRECT line, so she always sees how it should have
gone. Same scoring, same Mistake Dojo, same everything as the rest of Language practice.
A SMALL THING I WANT TO FLAG CLEARLY: I wrote all the Mandarin dialogue myself and have NOT had it
checked by a native speaker. You mentioned you speak Mandarin and can check things like this yourself --
I'd genuinely appreciate it if you had a look, especially the three "Talking to your teacher" exchanges
(they lean on 您/老师, the polite forms) and the family vocabulary. Nothing is live yet that depends on
it being perfect, so there's no rush, but I'd rather you caught anything wrong than Anna learning it
wrong.
A TEST GAP I FOUND AND CLOSED WHILE DOUBLE-CHECKING MY OWN WORK, BEING UPFRONT ABOUT IT: while doing my

STEPS
 1. Unzip this file so you can see the files (GitHub cannot open a zip).
 2. Go to github.com and open your repository, "Annaliesce-Academy-".
    You should see the file list, with index.html in it.
 3. Click  "Add file"  (near the top right of the file list), then  "Upload files".
 4. Drag index.html into the box (or click "choose your files"). It has the SAME NAME
    as the file already there, so GitHub replaces it automatically.
 5. Scroll down and click the green  "Commit changes"  button.
 6. Netlify notices by itself and updates the site in about a minute.
    To check: open Netlify, click your site, click "Deploys". The top one should say
    "Published".
 7. Open the site and refresh the page. If she has the site saved on her tablet or
    phone like an app, close it fully and open it again while online.
