THE REST OF THE CORE MATHS EXPLANATIONS NOW TEACH THE METHOD TOO
Last time I said area, angles, ratio, solving equations, circles and averages were still too terse. They are done. Every explanation is short numbered steps, ends on the answer, and where it helps checks the answer by working back:
 - AREA AND PERIMETER: says what area and perimeter MEAN (area counts squares, perimeter is the distance round the outside), works the multiplication by splitting a number over 10 (13 x 9 = 10 x 9 + 3 x 9), and explains a triangle as half of a rectangle.
   This was the "1/2 x 12 x 11 = 66" gap: it now shows 12 x 11 = 132, then half of 132 = 66.
 - POLYGONS: why the angles add up (draw lines from one corner, the shape splits into n - 2 triangles, each is 180 degrees), why a parallelogram is base x height (and to use the straight-up height, not the slanted side), and the trapezium as add, multiply by the height, halve.
 - ANGLES: 180 minus what you know, then a check that the angles add back up to 180.
 - RATIO: simplifying shows WHY a number goes into both sides. Recipes find the amount for ONE person first (150 / 5 = 30, checked by 30 x 5 = 150), then multiply. Sharing shows adding the parts, the size of one part, and checks that both shares add back to the total.
 - SOLVING EQUATIONS: says what the equation means in words ("7 lots of x, plus 7, makes 63"), undoes the steps backwards on both sides (63 - 7 = 56, then 56 / 7 = 8), and checks by putting x back in.
 - CIRCLES: explains diameter, radius, circumference and area, and splits the 3.14 multiplication into 3 x and 0.14 x so she can do it on paper (3 x 144 = 432 and 0.14 x 144 = 20.16, so 452.16).
 - AVERAGES: the mean shows adding up, dividing, and checking by multiplying back; the median shows putting them in order and taking the middle; the range shows the biggest and smallest.
So every one of the 32 core maths question types has now been read as a child who is stuck would read it, and either fixed or judged already clear.
How I checked: every equation in every explanation is re-worked independently, over thousands of questions (all 2,742 angle questions, all 1,320 equations, every circle radius, 1,500 random sets of averages...). I broke the code on purpose 10 ways; the tests caught 9 straight away and the 10th exposed a gap in my own test (it did not check the recipe multiplication), which I fixed and re-proved.

1. DECIMALS: EXPLANATIONS THAT ACTUALLY TEACH (what you reported)
You said Anna got 1.6 x 0.4 right but did not understand why the answer starts with 0, and got 53.1 / 9 wrong because the division itself was never shown. Both were true: the old explanations skipped the hard part.
Every Decimals explanation is now a short numbered working, one small step at a time, with the reason for each rule:
 - MULTIPLYING (1.6 x 0.4): multiply the whole numbers (16 x 4 = 64). WHY we count decimal places: turning 1.6 into 16 and 0.4 into 4 made each number 10 times bigger, so 64 is 100 times too big, so we move the point 2 places left.
   WHY THE 0 IS IN FRONT: 64 has exactly 2 digits, so the point lands at the very front with nothing before it, and we write a 0 there to show there are 0 whole ones and 64 hundredths. It also explains when extra 0s are needed (0.06)
   and why 0.30 is written 0.3. It ends with a quick estimate check (1.6 is about 2, and 2 x 0.4 = 0.8, so about 0.8 makes sense).
 - DIVIDING (53.1 / 9): a full bus stop (short division), digit by digit: where the decimal point goes, why 9 does not go into 5, 9 x 5 = 45, 53 - 45 = 8, carry the 8 to make 81, 9 x 9 = 81, then a check by multiplying back (5.9 x 9 = 53.1).
   If the number is shown as a whole number but the answer is not (like 3 / 2 = 1.5) it explains writing 3 as 3.0.
 - ADDING AND SUBTRACTING: line up the points, work column by column from the right, carrying and borrowing (including borrowing across a 0), then a quick check.
 - Every explanation now ends with a line like "So 53.1 / 9 = 5.9."
 - The hint says HOW, using her numbers: for 53.1 / 9 it says to set it out as a bus stop and start with how many 9s go into 53.
 - The "Why it may have gone wrong" line now checks the answer she ACTUALLY chose. If she picks 6.1: "6.1 x 9 = 54.9, but you started with 53.1, so 6.1 cannot be right." If the digits are right but the point is misplaced, it says the answer is 10 times too big or small.
 - When she gets one RIGHT, the explanation now also shows as numbered steps (before, only wrong answers did). Short explanations elsewhere are unchanged.
 - WRITTEN METHODS DIVISION (like 153 / 7) had the same problem ("7 x 21 = 147" with no way to find the 21). It now gets the same bus stop working, ending with the remainder.
 - The "method for this topic" card for Decimals now covers multiplying and dividing, not just adding.
How I checked it: by machine, every possible question of these kinds (all 384 multiplications, 472 divisions, 852 written divisions, and 8,000 adds and subtracts). Every number in every explanation is re-worked independently, and the digits written along the answer

LANGUAGE PRACTICE FOR SPANISH AND MANDARIN (the first part of queue item 10)
Where: open Spanish or Mandarin, then tap "Language practice" (next to Listening lab and Speaking practice).

 - Easy: short sentences, three choices, the English meaning is shown.
 - Medium: longer sentences, four choices, the meaning is shown.
 - Hard: the meaning is hidden (a hint shows it for 1 XP), and when she translates she picks the Spanish or Mandarin herself.
 - Extremely hard: long sentences, extra wrong words to avoid, Mandarin with NO pinyin, and vocabulary words are typed.
   Mandarin shows pinyin under the characters at Easy, Medium and Hard.

    To check: open Netlify, click your site, click "Deploys". The top one should say
    "Published".
 7. Open the site and refresh the page. If she has the site saved on her tablet or
    phone like an app, close it fully and open it again while online.
