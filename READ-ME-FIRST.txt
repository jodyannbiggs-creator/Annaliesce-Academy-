NEW IN VERSION 57 (included)

FIXED: A HINT COULD HAND OVER THE ANSWER FOR FREE
You spotted this. The second hint ("cross out wrong answers", 1 XP) always tried to cross out two — but on a question with only 3 choices (1 right, 2 wrong), crossing out both wrong ones just left
the correct answer sitting there for free. Now it always leaves at least one wrong answer on screen: a 3-choice question crosses out only one. This is one shared piece of code, so the fix applies to
every question in the Academy, not just one subject — including the language practice questions where you first noticed it.

HINTS ARE BECOMING SPECIFIC TO THE ACTUAL QUESTION, SUBJECT BY SUBJECT
You asked for hints to be specific to what she's actually being asked, not generic — using things she knows, like Roblox, where that genuinely fits. I checked the maths hints first: good news, maths
already does this and has for a while — every maths hint is already built from the real numbers in that exact question. So the work still to do is everywhere else. This version does two of them:
 - GEOGRAPHY, capital cities: every one of the 164 countries in the Academy now gets a real, specific clue instead of "it starts with the letter X". Your own example — capital of England — now hints
   "Big Ben, the Thames, and a famous Tower are all here." I checked every single clue by computer to make sure none of them ever gives away the actual answer, or points at one of the wrong choices
   on screen instead (a few clues had to be reworded because they accidentally named a country that was also one of the multiple-choice options).
 - SCIENCE, chemical symbols: all 28 elements now get a specific clue. Au and Fe (gold and iron) hint at Roblox Bedwars, since I checked that's genuinely where she'd know them from. I didn't force a
   Roblox link onto every element — most get a real, specific fact instead (bananas and potassium, toothpaste and fluorine, and so on), since making something up to sound personal would be worse.

STILL TO DO: you were clear this should be every subject, not just those two — I'd read it as an example rather than the whole ask. Counting properly, there are 33 more question types still using a

A BUG I FOUND AND FIXED, NOT RELATED TO THE LANGUAGE WORK
While testing, a wrong answer on a "what is the capital of ___" question would once in a while show as a fake multi-step "how to work it out" list instead of a plain fact. It only happened for
the United States, because "Washington, D.C." has two periods in it, and the app's code that turns an explanation into numbered steps was misreading "D.C." as the end of a sentence. In the worst
case it broke the explanation into five garbled pieces. I checked this also happened before this session's changes, so it was not something I introduced — I just happened to find it while testing.
Fixed by writing the capital as "Washington, DC" (no periods) everywhere it appears. This was not something you asked for; flagging it so you know it happened.

NEW IN VERSION 54 (included)

LANGUAGE PRACTICE FOR SPANISH AND MANDARIN (the first part of queue item 10)
Where: open Spanish or Mandarin, then tap "Language practice" (next to Listening lab and Speaking practice).

1. HOW HARD? Easy, Medium, Hard or Extremely hard. Each language keeps its own level.
 - Easy: short sentences, three choices, the English meaning is shown.
 - Medium: longer sentences, four choices, the meaning is shown.
 - Hard: the meaning is hidden (a hint shows it for 1 XP), and when she translates she picks the Spanish or Mandarin herself.
 - Extremely hard: long sentences, extra wrong words to avoid, Mandarin with NO pinyin, and vocabulary words are typed.
   Mandarin shows pinyin under the characters at Easy, Medium and Hard.

2. SOUND. Every question has a button to hear it, and there is a switch on the page to turn speaking off. It is remembered and shared between her devices.
 - With speaking off (or on a device with no Spanish or Mandarin voice) "Tap what you hear" becomes "Tap the words": she sees the English and builds the sentence.
 - On a "fill in the blank" question the sound button plays only AFTER she has answered, because saying the sentence would give the answer away.
 - It is the device's own voice. Nothing is sent anywhere.

3. THE GAMES (ten questions each, or a mix of all four)
 - FILL IN THE BLANK: a sentence with a gap. She picks the word that fits (verb endings, tu or usted, agreement, joining words, measure words in Mandarin).
 - TAP WHAT YOU HEAR: she hears the sentence and taps the words, in order, from a bank that also holds extra wrong words.
   A wrong answer shows which words were in the wrong place, then the right sentence and what it means.
 - TRANSLATE THE SENTENCE: Spanish or Mandarin to English at Easy and Medium; English to Spanish or Mandarin at Hard and Extremely hard.
 - TRANSLATE THE WORD: the usual drill, using the 124 Spanish and 74 Mandarin words already in the Academy.

4. HOW IT COUNTS
 - Right answers earn XP like any other round. Wrong answers go into the Mistake Dojo (added in version 55). Repeat after me (version 56) is in the Dojo too.
 - Every sentence belongs to a real lesson topic (Greetings, Family, Formal and informal and so on), so her answers count on the Coverage map.
 - The parent summary gets one new line: "Language practice: X of Y right".
 - 40 sentences in each language, 10 at each level.

THE LANGUAGE: the 80 sentences were written by me. The pinyin spelling and tone marks were checked against a dictionary, but no person has read the sentences yet (see the note under version 55).
NOT TESTED: the sound on a real device. I can only test with a pretend voice, so please try it on her device and tell me if anything sounds wrong.
